# Writing KDP Series Page Descriptions

KDP allows a series description (up to ~4000 chars) visible on the series landing page below individual book listings. Good series descriptions convert browsers into readers.

## Structure

**Opening hook (2-3 sentences):** Establish the world, stakes, and central question. Use concrete sensory language. Show the premise through action, not exposition.

**Thematic paragraph (2-3 sentences):** What the series is really about — not the plot, but the themes. Survival, sovereignty, discovery, generations. Connect the big idea to the reader's curiosity.

**Per-book blurbs (2-5 sentences each):** Each book gets a standalone mini-blurb. Structure:
- **Book I:** The beginning — who arrives, what's at stake, what they discover
- **Mid books:** The escalation — how the conflict grows, new characters, new questions
- **Final book:** The culmination — what's resolved, what's decided, the legacy

**Closing line (1 sentence):** Genre comps + thematic tag. "For fans of X and Y — a multi-generational epic about..."

## Tone

- Active voice, present tense for plot descriptions
- Concrete details over abstraction: "water squeezed from the dust" not "resource scarcity"
- Short sentences, varied rhythm — same standards as fiction prose
- No hype words (revolutionary, game-changing, groundbreaking)
- No plot-spoiler specifics — hint at stakes without revealing endings

## Character Count Management

KDP's series description limit is approximately 4000 characters. For a 5-book series:

| Section | Target chars |
|---------|-------------|
| Opening hook + thematic paragraph | ~800 |
| Book I blurb | ~400 |
| Book II blurb | ~350 |
| Book III blurb | ~350 |
| Book IV blurb | ~300 |
| Book V blurb | ~300 |
| Closing line | ~200 |
| Line breaks / formatting | ~300 |
| **Total** | **~3000** |

**Compaction techniques:**
- Remove articles (a, an, the) where grammar permits
- Condense "X is a novel of Y" → "A novel of Y"
- Merge adjacent short sentences with semicolons or em-dashes
- Remove redundant phrases ("the story of," "the tale of")
- Use one-sentence blurbs for middle books
- Cut genre comps to 2 max

## User Correction Pattern: Overly Long Descriptions

The most common user correction on series descriptions is **length**. If the user says "compact by N characters" without specifying what to cut:

**What the user actually means:** The prose is too flowery. Cut adjectives, consolidate repeated phrases, and tighten each book blurb to its narrative spine. Do not just remove spaces or line breaks.

**What to trim first (in order):**
1. **Thematic expansions** — the paragraph about "what the series explores" can usually lose 30-50% without losing meaning
2. **Book blurbs for middle books** — Books II, III, IV can often be 1-2 sentences instead of 2-4
3. **Genre comps** — cut from 3 to 2 max
4. **Repeated structural phrases** — don't say "A story of..." and "A novel of..." and "This book is..." in consecutive blurbs; use each once
5. **Opening hook adjectives** — remove two out of every three modifiers and check if the sentence still works

**Target compacted length for a 5-book series:** ~3000-3200 characters. Start here, then nibble down if the user says "still too long." Never write a first draft over 4000 characters.

**Pitfall:** Don't over-compact by removing the opening hook entirely — the hook is what sells the series. Prioritize cutting from middle books and thematic paragraphs before touching the opening or closing lines.

## Example Structure

```
**SERIES TITLE**

**Tagline (bold):** One sentence encapsulation.

Opening paragraph — world, stakes, central question.

Thematic paragraph — what the series explores.

---

**Book I — Title**

One-sentence opener. Story summary in 2-3 sentences. Thematic close.

---

**Book II — Title**

...

---

**Book V — Title**

Final book blurb — culmination, legacy.

---

Closing line — genre comps + thematic tag.
```
