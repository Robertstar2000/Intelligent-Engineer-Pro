# Editorial Spec Patterns — Reference Examples

This file documents the two major editorial spec formats the user provides for novella rewrites. Reference these when interpreting specs in future sessions.

## Pattern A: The 10-Item Improvement List (Cindy Lou rewrite)

The user provides improvements as a numbered list with emoji, label, and specific implementation guidance:

```
1. 🔥 Fix: Romantic tension
   How: Ted vs Bill — coffee vs croissants showdown, both asking her out at the end

2. 💔 Fix: Personal problem
   How: Failed dream firm interview, mom asking "When are you getting a real legal job?"

3. 😂 Fix: 10x more humor
   How: Bertha the dying plant dropping leaves in solidarity, forensic entomology jokes

4. 👭 Fix: Female friendship circle
   How: The Friday Night Legal Society — wine, legal pads, three generations of women

5. 🚨 Fix: Higher stakes
   How: Ted gets arrested; Cindy has to bail him out while the case teeters

6. 🎯 Fix: Personal villain
   How: Whitfield threatens Cindy's license — "I'm going to destroy you" — she fires back

7. 📈 Fix: Character arc
   How: Starts doubting, failed interviews → Ends: "Maybe I wasn't ready. I am now."

8. 🗽 Fix: NYC as character
   How: Odeon restaurant, bodega wine, 3 AM precinct, Bleecker Street, subway, bookshop

9. 😢 Fix: Emotional gut punch
   How: Mom says "I'm proud of you. Don't make me say it again."

10. 💕 Fix: Romantic ending
    How: Ted texts for dinner. Bill texts Carbone. Cindy: "Another complicated case."
```

**How to apply:** Each of the 10 items must be visible in the final output. They are not suggestions — they are requirements.

## Pattern B: The 5-Priority Rewrite (No Blue Sky reader magnet)

The user groups changes into 5 key priorities at the top:

```
important changes:
1. Add the family goodbye chapter.
2. Humanize Senna through vulnerability and personal sacrifice.
3. Strengthen the father/abandonment theme.
4. Add mission uncertainty and risk.
5. Make Earth itself a character readers will miss.
```

Then provides 25 detailed items below. The 5 priorities are the PRIMARY requirements. The 25 detailed items elaborate on HOW to implement them.

## Pattern C: The 44-Item Deep Rewrite (Last Transmission)

For the most comprehensive rewrites, the user provides a full structural breakdown organized by category:

| Section | Items | Focus |
|---------|-------|-------|
| Character | 6-10 items | Emotional wound, personal connection, risk of career, confidant |
| Worldbuilding | 4-6 items | Cosmic scale, fear, historical context, wonder |
| Structure | 4-5 items | Opening hook, transitions, emotional stakes before technical |
| Prose | 4-5 items | Sensory detail, show don't tell, subtext |
| Ending | 5-6 items | Launch tension, vulnerability, transformation, mystery seeding |

The "Master Revision List" format starts with `Open with...` or `First sentence should be...` as the very first instruction. No preamble.

## The Correction Lesson (Critical)

The user's single most important correction this session was:

> "Instead of adding the new features to the existing story you lost the experience."

This means: **The original story is the foundation. Every improvement must be woven INTO the existing narrative.** Do not replace original chapters with new content that covers the same ground differently. Do not remove original scenes to make room for new ones. Insert new scenes BETWEEN existing story beats. Expand existing scenes with new character interactions. Keep every original character introduction and scene.
